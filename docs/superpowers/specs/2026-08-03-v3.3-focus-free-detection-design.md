# v3.3 — focus-free change detection

Design, 2026-08-03. Revision 3. Status: **batya-approved, pending the owner's review**.

Changes what the PC agent's change-detection loop *looks at*. No wire format, no protocol version,
no change to which content wins.

---

## 1. The problem, as measured

The PC agent detects clipboard changes by forking `wl-paste --list-types` on every safety-net
tick. On this installation that call **takes keyboard focus** — Mutter implements no
`wlr-data-control`/`ext-data-control`, so a focus grab is how wl-clipboard reads a selection at
all.

| measurement | result |
|---|---|
| blind A/B, 2 Hz, 15 s per phase, phases unlabelled until the owner answered | `wl-paste --list-types` **blinks the foreground app**; `xclip -o -t TARGETS` on the same selection **does not** |
| passive process sampling, healthy mode, 60 s | **4 `wl-paste` forks** — one from the 30 s tick, the rest from a copy and GPaste's re-offer of it |
| `wl-paste --list-types` | **104 ms** |
| `GPaste2.GetElementAtIndex(0)` | **3–5 ms**, no focus |

The wl-paste phase is the **positive control**: it proves the observer could detect the effect, so
xclip's silence is a real negative rather than an insensitive instrument.

> **Correction, measured 2026-08-04 on the target machine: the 3–5 ms figure is not what the agent
> pays, and the two rows above are not comparable.** The agent invokes `gdbus` as a subprocess per
> call. Timed from a shell on the target, ten samples each:
>
> | call, as the agent actually makes it | wall clock |
> |---|---|
> | `gdbus … GPaste2.GetElementAtIndex 0` | **103–104 ms** |
> | `gdbus … Properties.Get GPaste2 Active` | **103–104 ms** |
> | `gdbus … Mutter.IdleMonitor.GetIdletime` | **103–104 ms** |
> | `gdbus … Peer.Ping` (does no work at all) | **103–105 ms** |
> | `/bin/true` (process-spawn floor) | **1–2 ms** |
>
> Every `gdbus` invocation costs the same ~103 ms regardless of the method, and `Peer.Ping` costs
> as much as `GetElementAtIndex`. **That ~103 ms is `gdbus`'s own startup — GIO init plus session
> bus connect — and the D-Bus round trip is lost in its noise.** The 3–5 ms above must have been
> measured as the round trip rather than as the CLI invocation.
>
> **This does not weaken the release; it relocates its justification.** The uuid probe is *not*
> 20–30× cheaper in wall clock than `wl-paste --list-types` — the two are within a millisecond of
> each other. The entire win is the one this document is named for: **the uuid probe does not steal
> focus and `wl-paste` does.** Any argument anywhere that leans on the uuid tier being cheap in
> time rather than free in focus is unsupported and should be reread against this table.
>
> Consequences to weigh at the final review rather than silently: the fast tier at 5 s pays ~2% duty
> cycle rather than the ~0.1% the 3–5 ms figure implies, and any gate that runs per tick on the 1 s
> degraded loop pays ~10%. Neither is disqualifying; both are an order of magnitude worse than
> planned, and the plan's "three orders of magnitude above the call's 3-5 ms cost" is now false.

### 1.1 The detection loop steals focus at the rate it polls

**The blink rate is the *probe* rate, not the read rate** — `probe()` runs every tick, `read()`
only when the token moves. Keystrokes are dropped while typing.

### 1.2 The safety net degrades on evidence that is systematically false — for images

`_observe_tick` arms when the probe token diverges while the accepted-signal counter has not
moved, and confirms on the next tick if it is still unmoved. Measured four times:

> **GPaste re-offers an image one to six seconds after a copy, changing the offered type list from
> one entry (`image/png`) to twenty-three — and emits no `Update` for it.** Exactly one Update per
> copy; none for the re-offer.

So the token moves for a reason that will never be signalled. When a tick boundary falls between
the Update and the re-offer, the net arms on a healthy machine and confirms one tick later.

**The Update→re-offer gap is what makes this rare**: measured at 4.6 s, 0.4 s, 0.03 s, 0.04 s. At
0.04 s a 30 s tick straddles it 0.13 % of the time; at 4.6 s, 15 %. The delay is *not* driven by
image size — a 3.1 MB image re-offered as fast as an 0.8 MB one; the driver is unknown.

### 1.3 Exactly three incidents exist, and only two are explained

Verified by counting the log rather than by recollection — three verdicts, ever:

| incident | signals | content | explained by §1.2? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `2026-08-01T10:06:37Z` | 2 | **text** | **NO — see below** |
| `2026-08-02T04:21:52Z` | 0 | image | yes — **reconstructed** to the second |
| `2026-08-02T13:24:03Z` | 18 | image | **consistent** with §1.2, *not* reconstructed |

**The text incident has no measured mechanism, and §1.2 does not supply one.** For text, `probe()`
returns the *body*, not the type list, and `trim-items` is `false`. Measured directly: a text
`wl-copy` produced **one** token change and **no** second one — GPaste's takeover of a text
selection leaves the body identical, so it cannot move that token.

Three readings remain open:

1. **A clip GPaste is configured not to see** — a password manager's sensitive hint, an exclusion
   pattern, a secret-class selection. GPaste adds none of these to its history and signals nothing
   for them **by construction, with tracking fully alive**. The selection body changes, no Update
   arrives, a tick arms, a quiet minute confirms. Nothing is broken. This is the most probable
   reading and the most testable, and it explains the ratio — one text case against two image
   ones, when the owner syncs screenshots constantly and copies passwords rarely.
2. **A true positive** — GPaste genuinely failed to signal a text change.
3. **Some other unsignalled mover of a text body** — unidentified.

**The design is invariant to which one is true, and that is the point.** Compare the costs under
the worst reading:

| | today | after v3.3 |
|---|---|---|
| what happens | the verdict fires: **permanent 1 Hz polling, blinking, dropped keystrokes** until reconnect | no verdict; the clip travels on the slow tier within 5–15 min |
| cost | a crippled desktop | one clip's latency |

**Under all three readings the new design is strictly better for the user, including the worst
one.** So the open question does not gate the release.

**Named cost, not discovered later:** the untracked class regresses in latency. A clip GPaste
refuses to track syncs within ≤30 s today via the net; afterwards it waits for a slow tick. A
deliberate trade, and the mirror of the win above.

**Consequence for the headline claim:** the uuid token would have prevented the **two image
incidents**. It is *not* established that it would have prevented all three. Stated at that
strength deliberately; an earlier draft said "all three".

### 1.4 Two facts that constrain any fix

**A powered-off monitor makes `wl-paste` hang.** Session `Active=yes`, `LockedHint=no`,
`ScreenSaver.GetActive=false`, and `wl-paste --list-types` times out entirely — even with no
selection owner — while `gpaste-client` answers in **0.102 s** and `gdbus introspect` exits 0.
Monitor on: **104 ms**. Mechanism inferred, not verified.

This retro-explains the production log's `3.004 s` timeouts at **exactly 33 s spacing** — a 30 s
tick plus a 3 s timeout: the safety net timing out against itself with the screen off, with
`read_ok = None` correctly holding the verdict locked throughout.

**GPaste's query path answers while its signal path is silent.** Directly measured at **one**
incident — `2026-08-02T13:24:03Z`, where the daemon answered in **0.104 s** with the extension
enabled while the verdict declared the source dead — and again at **0.102 s** during this
release's own monitor-off session. For the other two incidents the daemon is only known to have
been `Active` from the journal; the latency there is **extrapolated, not measured**. The claim is
load-bearing for §3, so it is stated at the strength the evidence actually supports.

## 2. Scope

**In:** the uuid fast tier; the gated slow `wl-paste` tier **with its own verdict rule** (§4.2,
not deferrable); the failed-read rule and its backoff (§5.1); the uuid-tier-dead state (§4.3); the
two-way state split (§6); the harness capabilities that make §9 runnable (§9.0); a documentation
audit (§8).

**Out, deliberately:**

- **The stuck latch** (un-degrade on signal evidence; rebuild the subscription). Next cycle.
  Justified because after this change the *common* false verdict is gone and the remaining one is
  far cheaper (§6.1).
- **Reading content from GPaste.** Reads stay on `wl-paste` — §7.
- **`xclip`** — §2.1.
- **The standalone `PollingWatcher` path is not touched at all.** A machine with no GPaste keeps
  today's behaviour exactly. The uuid tier lives only in the `GPasteWatcher` branch of
  `make_watcher`. Stated explicitly because this project has already shipped a fix sited in a
  watcher that is never constructed on the target machine; this is the mirror of that trap, and
  the same sentence prevents both.

### 2.1 xclip — measured, and deliberately not used

Focus-free (§1), and that is now measured rather than assumed. Still not adopted: ~~104 ms against
3–5 ms~~ — **that comparison is retracted, see §1's correction: measured as the agent invokes them,
both cost ~103 ms and the difference is focus, not time**; needs `XAUTHORITY` at a path whose suffix
is regenerated per session, which the agent —
spawned by `sshd`, outside the session — would have to go hunting for; returns a different type
list through the bridge; adds an apt dependency to a single-file agent; and **does not fix §1.2**,
because the re-offer moves the X type list too.

Its one unique property is real: it is the **only measured focus-free observer of the selection
itself**, blind in neither failure mode. If §6.2 is ever actually observed in production, it is
the first thing to reach for — recorded in the ledger in those words so nobody re-runs the A/B to
rediscover it. Paying an apt dependency *now*, for a state never once observed, inverts the burden
of proof.

## 3. Why GPaste may police GPaste

The **signal path** is a broadcast subscription — a match rule, a child process, a pipe. The
**query path** is a request-reply method call. Mechanically different channels with different
failure modes, demonstrated from the wrong side at the one incident where the latency was actually
measured (§1.4) and consistent with the journal at the other two.

The one failure they share is a wedged daemon, and that one fails **loudly**: a method-call
timeout is evidence, unlike a signal that merely never comes.

*Not claimed:* that the uuid is the only candidate immune to §1.2. A history-file mtime token
would also not have moved during either image incident. The uuid wins on other axes — it is a
positive identifier rather than a timestamp, needs no knowledge of GPaste's on-disk layout, and
**takes no focus** — not on uniqueness, and (§1's correction) **not on cost either: it is within a
millisecond of the probe it replaces.**

## 4. The design

### 4.0 The state table — the machine, stated once

Every rule below refers to this. **The slow tier's divergence predicate is not "the wl-paste token
moved while signals were silent"** — that is today's predicate, and reusing it would make the slow
tier declare "tracking dead" during §6.1, where signals *are* silent and the wl-paste token *does*
move on real copies. It compares against the **fast tier**, and against nothing else:

| uuid | wl-paste token | reading |
|---|---|---|
| moves | — | tracking alive. Whether the *signal path* is alive is a separate question, answered by the signal counter alone: arriving → healthy; silent → **§6.1** |
| **frozen** | **moves** | **§6.2 candidate** — tracking dead |
| frozen | frozen | no evidence either way; nothing arms |
| **unknown** (method failing) | anything | **no verdict may be reached — see below** |

**Signals are deliberately absent from the §6.2 predicate.** Operations on the history alone —
deleting an entry from GPaste's UI — emit `Update` without any selection tracking whatever, so a
signal is *not* evidence that tracking is alive. The uuid/wl-paste pair is, and only it. Do not
later "strengthen" this predicate with an `and signals silent` clause.

### 4.0.1 "Frozen" means measured-and-unchanged, never unmeasured

**This distinction is load-bearing, and getting it wrong produces a permanent false §6.2 verdict.**

- **Frozen** = the method call **succeeded** and returned the **same** uuid as last time.
- A method **failure** yields `None`: it feeds no predicate, on either tier. It is not "frozen".

Without this, a failing `GetElementAtIndex` plus an active user reads as "uuid frozen while
wl-paste moves" — the §6.2 signature — and the slow tier declares tracking dead, latching (the
latch is out of scope for this release, so it never clears), all because a method was renamed.
It would reach that verdict *before* §4.3's failure run is long enough to trigger the fallback.

**Therefore: while a method-failure run is in progress, the slow tier reaches no verdict at all.**
Once §4.3's fallback engages, this table stops applying and today's mechanics resume.

### 4.1 Fast tier — the GPaste history uuid

`GetElementAtIndex(0)` → the uuid, compared like today's token; the worker still does the
authoritative `wl-paste` read when it moves.

**The interval is a named budget:** the worst-case PC→Mac latency in state §6.1. The subscription
stays the **primary** source and its latency stays effectively zero — the uuid tier must not
silently supersede it. 5 s is below the human threshold for noticing clipboard lag and three
orders of magnitude above the call's cost.

A single method error is a `None` probe — evidence about nothing, which resets an armed run,
mirroring today's `read_ok`. Persistent failure is a different thing entirely: §4.3.

**Baseline on a watcher rebuild.** A new watcher takes its uuid baseline on its first tick, so a
copy landing inside the rebuild window is absorbed by the baseline silently. This is *today's*
behaviour, not a uuid regression, and it is covered by reconciliation on reconnect but not on a
mid-connection Wayland flap. Said here so an implementer does not "fix" it in passing and open an
echo race.

### 4.2 Slow tier — the gated `wl-paste` probe and its own verdict rule

Needed because the uuid lies by being frozen when GPaste stops tracking — and, per §1.3, possibly
in the one incident we cannot explain.

**It consumes `wl-paste` tokens, so it inherits §1.2's false-positive input.** A takeover
straddling a slow tick reproduces the identical false verdict on a longer fuse, behind the same
latch that never clears. **This is why its verdict rule cannot be deferred along with the latch:**
it is not a separate improvement, it is what makes the two-tier design safe.

- **Divergence is defined by §4.0** — wl-paste token moved *while the uuid stayed frozen*.
- **A settled token clears an armed run.** On the fast tier's input this would be wrong, and the
  existing comment argues so correctly *for that input*. Here it is inverted: unsignalled-then-
  settling is a **known benign class** (GPaste's takeover), while a dead tracker under an active
  user produces **repeated** divergence.
- **On divergence, re-probe within seconds** instead of waiting a full slow interval. A takeover
  settles; a dead tracker persists.

**Two gates, which matter more than the interval — without them no interval is defensible:**
the monitor is on (else every tick is a guaranteed 3 s timeout into nothing), and the user has
been recently active via `Mutter.IdleMonitor.GetIdletime` (with nobody copying there is no
divergence to find, so an idle blink is pure cost). With both, 5–15 minutes is justified.

> **Shipped, and it is ONE gate and not 5–15 minutes. Recorded 2026-08-04.**
>
> **The idle gate shipped as written** — `GPasteWatcher._slow_tier_should_probe`,
> `USER_IDLE_GATE_SECONDS = 300.0`, and a `None` reading proceeds rather than gating.
>
> **The monitor gate did not, and its substitution was deliberate.** `PowerSaveMode`'s
> monitor-**off** branch was never measured, and §1.4's own mechanism for the hang is a hotplug
> disconnect rather than DPMS — under which the property may well stay `0` while the output list
> changes instead. What stands in its place is Task 8's **timeout backoff**: a slow-tier probe
> that times out backs the tier off toward `SAFETY_NET_POLL_SECONDS`. It needs no new API, has no
> unmeasured branch, and covers *every* cause of a hanging `wl-paste` including a locked session,
> which `PowerSaveMode` would miss entirely. The plan's implementation note carries the full
> reasoning; `PowerSaveMode` with the monitor off is on the hardware-acceptance list so a future
> cycle can adopt it as the cheap early exit this paragraph wanted.
>
> **The 5–15 minute interval is a TARGET, not shipped.** `make_watcher` passes no
> `safety_net_interval_seconds`, so every production slow tier runs at
> `SAFETY_NET_POLL_SECONDS = 30.0`. That is the conservative choice rather than an oversight, and
> for a reason neither this section nor the plan had: **lengthening the tier has an ambiguous
> effect on the excluded-clip false-positive class.** A longer window is likelier to contain an
> unrecorded clip (which arms) *and* likelier to contain an intervening tracked copy (which
> clears the run). The net is unmeasured. 30 s stands pending hardware acceptance; whoever raises
> it owns all three interval-inversion sites the plan's follow-up ticket names, not just this
> sentence.

**The slow tier always feeds the worker**, not only the judge, so clips GPaste fails to track
still sync at the slow cadence. **Its cost, stated rather than discovered:** with an active user,
a slow tick following a copy sees its own rare baseline move and provokes one extra full read.
That read is absorbed by `_last_seen`/echo suppression — it costs a fork, not a defect.

### 4.3 The uuid tier itself failing — a third state

`available()` passes and the daemon answers, but `GetElementAtIndex` fails *persistently* — a
renamed method, an older interface, anything. Treating each error as a `None` probe is right for
an episode and catastrophic as a steady state: the token never moves, the fast tier is mute, and
detection silently falls to the slow tier's 5–15 minute cadence — a 30–180× degradation of sync
with **not one line in the log**.

Rule: **N consecutive method failures → one log line naming the observed cause → fall back to
today's behaviour** (the `wl-paste` net at 30 s). Named, not guessed — see §5.3.

**N is defined, not left to the plan:** enough consecutive failures to span two slow-tier ticks,
so the fallback engages before the slow tier could have judged anything on stale information. With
a 5 s fast tick and a 5 min slow tick that is well over a hundred, so express it as a *duration*
(≥ 2 × the slow interval) rather than a raw count, and let the count follow from the interval.
Until it engages, §4.0.1 already bars a verdict — N sets when the log line and the fallback
arrive, not whether the machine is safe meanwhile.

**Recovery from this fallback — the method starting to answer again — is next cycle's latch
work**, the same family as un-degrading on signal evidence. Deliberately not closed here, so the
plan does not close it in passing.

## 5. Rules that are easy to lose

### 5.1 A failed read must not consume a token advance — and must back off

Today the probe fails *together* with the read, so when the screen returns the `None → value`
transition **re-signals** and the missed copy is caught up.

With a uuid the probe **succeeds while the read hangs**. If the baseline advances against a failed
read, the change is consumed-but-not-synced and **lost permanently**.

**But the naive rule causes a retry storm worse than today.** "The baseline advances only when an
observation reached a decision" means: the read hangs (monitor off after a copy) → baseline not
advanced → the next 5 s tick re-signals → another read → another 3 s timeout. **Twelve timeout
forks a minute against today's two**, in exactly the scenario the rule was written for.

So the rule is two-part, and both parts are **required** — not a choice:

1. An unresolved observation does not advance the baseline.
2. **Re-signalling for it backs off** — mandatory, because it also covers hangs that have nothing
   to do with the monitor (a locked session, for one).
3. The monitor gate of §4.2 applies **on top**, as a cheap early exit — not as a substitute for
   the backoff.

Today's structure gets the retry by accident; this must reproduce it deliberately *and* bound it.

> **Correction, 2026-08-04: shipped, the backoff IS the substitute, and point 3 has it exactly
> backwards.** No monitor gate was built — see §4.2's correction block for why — so there is no
> "on top" layer, and points 1 and 2 carry the whole job on their own. The sentence matters
> because the shipped source cites it: `_slow_tier_should_probe`'s counter-argument paragraph
> answers "a gated tick could save a 3 s timeout" with this point, and has been corrected to cite
> the backoff directly instead.

### 5.2 Never log the probe's payload

`GetElementAtIndex(0)` returns `(uuid, text)` — the top item's text, possibly a password or a
private URL, crossing a subprocess stdout every 5 s. **Parse the uuid only; never log the value,
including in exception branches.** Today's token never appears in a log line; that must stay true.

### 5.3 Name the cause, do not guess it

Read GPaste's tracking-state property over D-Bus and put the observed value in the log line. The
current verdict line blames a disabled gnome-shell extension; the extension was measured **enabled
and ACTIVE** during all three incidents. That makes three shipped guesses.

> **Correction, measured 2026-08-04 on the target machine — this section originally said
> `Tracking`, and there is no such property.** Introspecting `org.gnome.GPaste` at
> `/org/gnome/GPaste` on GPaste **45.3** gives interface `org.gnome.GPaste2` with
> `Track(in b tracking-state)` as a **method** and `readonly b Active` as the **property**.
> `Properties.Get org.gnome.GPaste2 Active` returns `(<true>,)` in **103 ms**;
> `Properties.Get org.gnome.GPaste2 Tracking` fails with
> `org.freedesktop.DBus.Error.InvalidArgs: No such property "Tracking"`, **exit 1, empty stdout**.
>
> So the property to read is **`Active` on `org.gnome.GPaste2`**. Had this section shipped as
> written, every production verdict line would have reported the field as unavailable — the
> deliverable would have been inert, which is the same "a mechanism ships and nothing exercises it"
> shape §5.3 exists to end. Task 12's implementer predicted this from GPaste's published
> introspection *before* it was measured, implemented the section as written rather than building
> an unmeasured two-call hedge, and disclosed it; the measurement above is what settled it.
>
> The wrong-name failure mode is worth keeping on the record because it is what made shipping the
> unverified name safe: a bad property name exits non-zero with empty stdout, so the reader reports
> "not measured" and never a guess.

## 6. The state splits, and both halves need names

`DEGRADED_POLL_SECONDS` stops meaning one thing.

**Who inherits the existing latch, stated so the plan does not decide it by convenience.** Today
`Agent._event_source_degraded` and the `on_degrade` chain serve one verdict; now there are two.

- **The latch belongs to §6.2 only** — it is what carries "tracking is dead" across a watcher
  rebuild, which is what it was written for.
- **§6.1 gets its own one-shot flag, and it is a *log* flag, not a mode latch.** It suppresses a
  repeated log line and nothing else: §6.1 changes no interval, so a stuck flag there costs a
  missing log line, not a crippled desktop.

### 6.1 Signal path silent, tracking alive — *not degraded*

The fast tier **is** the sync, at 5 s, zero focus. A log line, not a mode change. Today this state
drops the machine to 1 Hz focus-stealing polling; afterwards it costs seconds of latency and
nothing else. **This is what makes deferring the latch acceptable.**

### 6.2 Tracking dead — genuinely degraded

The only state where a `wl-paste` interval is still a focus-steal knob, and the only place blinking
survives this design. **It must not be today's flat 1 s.** Backoff with a named shape — start
~5 s, cap at today's 30 s, reset on an observed change — plus the idle gate. Named here so the
plan does not invent its own.

---

## 7. Non-goal: reading content from GPaste

"Detection survives a powered-off monitor" is true. "Sync survives it" is **not**: delivery still
goes through a `wl-paste` read, which hangs. The half-truth is stated because it invites a scope
inflation that must be refused: `GetElementAtIndex` may return a shortened *display* string, raw
content is a separate call, images are not available over the bus, and the project's central rule
— *hash the bytes you read back* — is justified against what `wl-paste` returns.

## 9. Acceptance

### 9.0 Harness capabilities this requires — scope, not assumption

§9.1 is unrunnable today, and saying so is part of the design. Three capabilities must be built:

1. **A takeover mode in `fake_clipboard.py` triggered by a *probe counter*, not by time** —
   "change the offered type list immediately after the Nth probe", emitting no signal. The fake
   sits between agent and clipboard and therefore sees probe calls; **shortening intervals alone
   would only make the straddle probable, never deterministic.** Four live attempts failed for
   exactly this reason.
2. **A fake `gdbus` answering `GetElementAtIndex` from a controllable history** — without it the
   uuid tier cannot run under the harness at all.
3. **Interval injection** into the agent spawned by the harness.

### 9.1–9.7 The criteria

1. The harness reproduces §1.2 deterministically against the **old** token and shows it gone with
   the new one.
2. **A mutually exclusive test pair, each named by its reason** — the structural guard against a
   later release merging the two verdict rules back into one:
   - fast tier: *a settled token must confirm*, or a dead source on a machine with sparse copies
     is never diagnosed;
   - slow tier: *a settled token must clear*, or GPaste's takeover yields a false verdict.
   Merging the rules would require deliberately deleting one of these.

   > **Corrected 2026-08-04: the fast half cannot be written as worded, because the fast tier
   > reaches no verdict at all.** §9.8's two-verdict-function shape was deliberately not built
   > (see its own correction block), so there is no "fast tier verdict" for a settled token to
   > confirm. The guard survives with the same force, re-expressed against what shipped — **the
   > slow tier's clears-rule must not be mergeable into the fast tier's behaviour**:
   >
   > - **fast tier**: a *moved* uuid signals and a *frozen* one signals nothing —
   >   `test_a_moved_uuid_signals`, `test_a_frozen_uuid_does_not_signal`. Frozen is not evidence
   >   here; it is the absence of any.
   > - **slow tier**: a *settled* token **clears** an armed run while repeated divergence
   >   **confirms** — `test_a_takeover_does_not_degrade`, `test_a_dead_tracker_still_degrades`.
   >
   > Merging the rules still requires deliberately deleting one of these: either the slow tier
   > starts confirming on a settled token (which is the pre-v3.3 defect this release exists to
   > remove) or the fast tier starts treating a frozen uuid as evidence of something. Both tests
   > name their reason in their own docstring, which is what the item asked for.
3. §5.1 has a test where the read fails while the token advances — the change must still sync —
   **and** one asserting the retry backs off rather than storming.
4. §4.3 has a test: persistent method failure produces the log line and the fallback.
5. No log line ever contains the probe's payload.
6. Hardware acceptance: the passive 60 s process count drops from **4 `wl-paste` forks** to the
   copy-driven ones only. **Qualified, because the bare claim is false**: with the slow tier at
   5 min and an active user, a legitimate slow tick lands in a 60 s window ~20 % of the time. So:
   the slow tier disabled for the measurement, or a window long enough to carry a stated expected
   count. Plus a 2 Hz A/B against the new tier reporting no blinking.

   > **The qualification names the wrong variable, and this is corrected BEFORE anyone runs it
   > (2026-08-04).** It is written about the slow *interval*, but the interval that shipped is

### 9.8 Implementation shape, because it is load-bearing

**Two functions, named by the nature of their input** — a verdict over a signalled token, a
verdict over a takeover-prone token — not one function with a `clears`/`confirms` parameter.
Merging them should require a deliberate rename, not a flag flip.

> **Correction, 2026-08-04: this shape was deliberately NOT built, and the spec is what needs
> correcting rather than the code.** There is **one** verdict function, `_observe_tick`, and one
> **signaller**, `_fast_tick`, which reaches no verdict at all — it sets the shared event when the
> uuid moves and logs §6.1 once, and that is the whole of it. So the second of the two functions
> this section asks for has no verdict to hold, and there is no `clears`/`confirms` parameter

> **The guard this section wanted survives, in a different place.** What §9.8 was protecting is

> that wanted to merge them would still have to delete a named test, which is what "a deliberate
