# clipwire v3.2 — the PC says where its bytes came from

**Date:** 2026-08-01
**Status:** draft, pending batya review
**Amends:** [2026-07-31-protocol-v3-images-design.md](2026-07-31-protocol-v3-images-design.md)
and supersedes the density half of
[2026-08-01-v3.1-loop-and-density-design.md](2026-08-01-v3.1-loop-and-density-design.md).
The clip-state frame gains one optional field.

## The bug, stated correctly for the first time

A retina screenshot copied on the Mac comes back from the PC pasting at double size.

Previous framings called this "GPaste drops `pHYs`". That is the mechanism of the *damage*,
not of the *delivery*, and getting them confused is what produced two failed fixes. The
delivery is ours:

1. The Mac sends an image stamped `T`.
2. The PC applies it and arms `_expect_reoffer`.
3. GPaste takes the selection and re-encodes. `_consume_image_reoffer` recognises this as the
   agent's own write coming back changed, **deliberately sends nothing** — the content is
   already on the peer in its original encoding — and records `sha256(re-encode)` at
   **`T + REOFFER_TS_NUDGE_SECONDS`**.
4. At the next reconnect the PC holds `(H_reencode, T+1ms)` against the Mac's `(H_original, T)`.
   `T+1ms > T`, so the PC wins and sends the re-encode. The Mac's own screenshot comes back
   degraded.

**So the bug and the nudge are one decision seen from two sides.** The nudge exists because
stamping the peer's timestamp *exactly* leaves both sides at an equal `ts` holding different
bytes, which hands the reconnect to the hex tie-break — a coin flip, and v3 measured that the
losing side re-sends forever. The nudge fixed that by making the PC win **deterministically**.
Winning deterministically is exactly what delivers the degraded copy, every time.

That is not an argument for removing the nudge. Its reasoning is sound and its bounds are
worked out. It is an argument that **freshness is the wrong question here**, because both
sides hold the same picture and no ordering of two timestamps can say so.

## Why nothing that compares content can work

v3.1 tried comparing decoded pixels, twice, and both are now measured dead:

| | differing bytes | max delta |
|---|---|---|
| re-tag as sRGB (shipped) | 398,267 of 614,400 | 20 |
| convert into sRGB (replaced) | 18,772 of 614,400 | 1 |

GPaste applies the embedded ICC profile on load and writes untagged, so the samples genuinely
move. Neither strategy reaches byte equality, and a tolerance would be a threshold calibrated
on one sample of one unspecified program.

The deeper reason is structural: **every content comparison infers provenance, lossily, from a
fact the PC knew directly and discarded.** It wrote the Mac's bytes and read different ones
back with nobody touching the clipboard in between. It is the only witness, and it threw away
its testimony.

## The fix: announce the origin, do not deduce it

When `_consume_image_reoffer` records the re-encode, it also records **the hash it was given**.
Clip-state gains an optional `origin` field carrying it.

**One small correction to the obvious plan, found by reading rather than assuming.**
`_expect_reoffer` does not currently hold that hash — it holds only the peer's timestamp
(`self._expect_reoffer = ts if kind == KIND_IMAGE else None`, `agent/clipwire-agent.py:1237`).
It must carry the pair. The hash is already in scope one line above, where `_last_seen` is set
from the same write, so this is widening a tuple rather than plumbing a new value through: the
bytes the PC writes are the bytes the Mac hashed and sent, so their digest **is** the peer's
canonical hash.

The rule is **one** pure function, `resolve_provenance(mine, theirs)`, beside
`resolve_freshness`, and both sides call the same one:

- `theirs.origin == mine.sha256` — the peer's content was born from mine. Do not wait for it
  and do not accept it.
- `mine.origin == theirs.sha256` — the peer holds my ancestor. Do not send it my derivative.

Either match resolves `DO_NOTHING`. Both sides stand down and both stores stay honest about
what their own clipboard holds.

**Both comparisons live in one function, not one per side.** Describing it as "the Mac's rule"
and "the PC's rule" is fine as prose; implementing it that way is this project's recorded
defect shape — two implementations of one idea drifting apart. It gets a shared golden fixture,
`fixtures/provenance.json`, beside `fixtures/freshness.json`, and both suites read it.

**Equality only counts between two values that are both present.** `None == None` is `True` in
Python and `nil == nil` is `true` for a Swift `Optional`, and the naive rule would therefore
fire on **an empty clipboard against a peer with no origin** — a locked PC, which happens
daily, against an ordinary Mac. Both sides would stand down and `(_, nil) → sendMine`, the
recovery for a peer that lost its clipboard, would be dead **for every kind of content**. That
is the v3.1 review's finding resurrected one layer up.

So: preemption requires two present, equal values. On the wire `origin` is admissible only
beside a non-null `sha256`, rejected in decode the way `kind` already is — nil-iff-nil, checked
rather than assumed.

**This runs before the freshness comparison**, not inside it. `resolve_freshness` stays exactly
the formula it has been since v2, pinned by `fixtures/freshness.json`, which this release must
not touch.

### What it costs, and what it removes

**On the wire:** one optional field. Absent means today's behaviour, so a mismatched pair
degrades to exactly what ships now rather than failing. Both sides already ignore unknown
keys — verified during v3.1 — so an old peer reading a new clip-state is unaffected.

**In the store:** v3.1's `StoredClipState.localSHA256` becomes unnecessary and is **deleted**.
Under provenance the Mac never adopts the peer's hash as canonical; it simply declines the
transfer, so both sides return to one hash describing their own clipboard. The `sendMine`
verification returns to comparing `mine.sha256`.

That deletion is free, and the reason is worth stating: **the v3.1 fix never fired on any real
machine, so no store anywhere carries `localSHA256`.** There is nothing to migrate.

**In the comparison:** `imagePixelsIdentical`, `imagePixelDifference` and `ImagePixels.swift`
go with it. Provenance needs no decoder at all, which is also why it survives transformations
that no comparison could — including a lossy one, and including whatever the next GPaste does.

## The origin's whole life, because the rule alone is not enough

An earlier draft gave the comparison and left everything else implied. Each of the three gaps
below is a way for this fix to be inert or harmful, and the first two are how it would fail
**silently for the third time**.

### It has to survive the agent's death, or the fix is inert

`sshd` starts a new agent per connection, so the process that recorded an origin **is already
dead** at the one moment the origin is needed: the announce at the next reconnect. A field that
only ever lives in memory is worth nothing here.

So the persistent store becomes a quadruple — `(sha256, ts, kind, origin)` — `save_clip_state`
and `load_clip_state` carry it, and **`resolve_startup_state`'s match branch returns the stored
record whole** rather than rebuilding it around the current hash. Rebuilding drops the origin,
and the fix then dies on the *second* reconnect, which is exactly the shape the harness's
two-reconnect test exists to catch.

An old store file with no `origin` key loads with `origin=None`, the way
`TestV2StoreIsRejected` (`agent/tests/test_clip_state_store.py:710`) already establishes for a
missing `kind`. There is a precedent to follow rather than a policy to invent.

### It has to die with the content it describes, or it silences a real clip

A stale origin is not harmless in both directions. The safe direction is the one that is easy
to think of — an origin naming a hash the peer no longer holds simply never matches. **The
mirror is a silent loss:** the PC holds new user content `U` with a stale `origin = M`, while
the Mac still holds `M`. Then `theirs.sha256 (M) == mine.origin (M)` fires, and the PC **never
sends `U`**. A clip the user copied, gone, on an entirely routine path.

So:

- every store write **outside** the recording branch writes `origin = None`
- `resolve_startup_state`'s **changed** branch clears it
- its **matched** branch keeps it

That is precisely the preserve-on-match / drop-on-change pair this release **deletes** from the
Mac along with `localSHA256`. The pattern is not new and it was not wrong — it was on the wrong
side of the wire. It moves to the side that actually witnesses the substitution.

### Why images only

Text does not need this: GPaste's text substitution is the `trim-items` whitespace strip, which
the README already documents and which the owner has turned off — and its shape is different,
because a trimmed text clip goes back over the live connection rather than waiting for a
reconnect. The boundary is drawn where the two behaviours actually differ, not by analogy.

## The rule about when to record an origin

Only when the re-offer is genuinely a re-offer: the read arrived on the first observation after
our own write, and the kind matches. Otherwise a clip the user copied inside that window gets
labelled a derivative of the peer's content and is silently never synced.

`_consume_image_reoffer` already distinguishes those cases — its three `True` branches are
exactly this discrimination, and only the first consumes the expectation. **The origin is
recorded on that branch and nowhere else.**

### The inherited hazard is made WORSE, and that has to be handled

An earlier draft of this section claimed the existing hazard was "inherited, not widened".
That was wrong, and the correction matters more than the claim did.

The hazard: on an installation with no GPaste the re-offer never comes, so an expectation armed
by an applied image stays armed until the user's **next** image copy, which is absorbed as
though it were the re-offer. Today that clip is **recovered at the next reconnect** — the store
carries it at `T+1ms`, the PC wins freshness, and it goes to the Mac one connection late.

With an origin stamped on it, the Mac holds the ancestor the origin names, provenance fires,
and both sides stand down. **A clip that was late becomes a clip that is gone.** v3.2 would
turn a temporary loss into a permanent one.

So the expectation gets disarmed if the re-offer does not arrive within one safety-net poll —
30 seconds, against a takeover measured at 1–4 seconds. v3 deliberately rejected a timer here,
and that decision was right **under the costs as they then stood**: a false disarm cost one
extra frame, and a false absorption cost one late clip. The costs have now inverted — a false
absorption costs a clip permanently — so the reasoning that rejected the timer now argues for
it. The ruling is attached to its premise, again.

## Acceptance

1. **A retina screenshot survives a reconnect at its original display size**, measured on the
   real machines the way the failure was measured — `pixelsWide`/`pixelsHigh` against
   `NSImage.size`, before and after. This is the one that matters; the bug has now outlived two
   fixes that passed everything else.
2. **The reconnect resolves `doNothing` on both sides**, with no frame sent in either
   direction.
3. **No false `clipboard changed while apart`** on any reconnect afterwards.
4. **`fixtures/freshness.json` is untouched**, and both freshness implementations still agree
   with it. Provenance runs before that formula and must not perturb it.
5. **An old peer still works.** A clip-state without `origin` behaves exactly as it does today,
   in both directions.
6. **The harness proves it in the loop**, using the fake that now moves every pixel sample —
   which no content comparison can pass, and provenance does not need to.
7. **An empty clipboard against a peer with no origin still recovers.** Two absent values must
   not compare equal; `(_, nil) → sendMine` must still fire. This is the daily locked-PC path.
8. **An origin survives an agent restart.** Record one, kill the agent, reconnect twice, and
   confirm it still suppresses on the second reconnect as well as the first.
9. **A stale origin does not silence a real clip.** New content on the PC with the Mac still
   holding the ancestor must still be sent.
10. **The provenance rule is one function with one fixture**, `fixtures/provenance.json`, read
    by both suites — not two implementations that agree today.
11. **`localSHA256` and `ImagePixels.swift` are gone**, and the suites do not lose a test to
   their removal that was pinning behaviour rather than pinning them.
