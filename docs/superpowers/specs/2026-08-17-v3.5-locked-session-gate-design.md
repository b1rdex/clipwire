# v3.5 — a locked session queues nothing

Date: 2026-08-17. Amends v3.4 (which remains the authority on the focus-free text tier) and the
design doc's connection-lifecycle section. Status: batya-reviewed; three blockers and five
corrections applied, recorded in §10.

Every number in this document was measured on the target machine on 2026-08-17 unless it cites an
earlier spec by section. Where something is inferred rather than measured, the sentence says so.

---

## 1. The incident, and what measuring it changed

The PC's GNOME session stayed locked for ≤17.5 h while the owner worked on the Mac. On unlock, the
shell lagged and the app panel showed a crowd of "Unknown" tiles. The Mac's log for the whole
window is silent — one connection held for 22 h, zero reconnects.

There is no queue anywhere in the protocol, and that is precisely the mechanism. The Mac sends
every observed change immediately (`Sources/clipwire/Pasteboard.swift:345`); the agent applies each
inline (`_on_clip` → `_write_clip`, `agent/clipwire-agent.py:389,514`); every apply spawns one
`wl-copy` detached, with no timeout and no wait, resident-by-design as the next selection owner
(`agent/clipwire-agent.py:941-965`). Measured on 2026-08-17, session locked (`LockedHint=yes`):

- Each such spawn **hangs without taking the selection**. Two agent-replica spawns → two resident
  processes, one per clip. The pile grows 1:1 with copies. Each is a Wayland client holding the
  surface it needs for a focus serial (v3.4 §1.1) — one "Unknown" tile per copy.
- On `loginctl unlock-session` the pile resolved in **under one second** — and was recorded into
  GPaste's history in **reverse spawn order**. The clipboard a user returns to holds the **oldest**
  clip of the locked stretch, not the newest, and the day's copies land in the history at unlock,
  evicting the owner's own entries toward the 1000-item cap.
- `wl-paste --list-types` times out under lock (rc=124 at the 3 s cap), which is the burst signature
  the README already documents.

Two asymmetries, both load-bearing:

- **Writes and reads break under different conditions.** The hung writes resolved at the unlock
  *edge* even though the monitor stayed dark; reads kept timing out until the screen was lit.
  Writes are broken by the lock alone; reads by the lock *or* a dark monitor (v3.3's measurement,
  reproduced today). A `LockedHint` gate is therefore the *right* signal for writes and would be
  the wrong one for reads — this spec gates writes and does not touch read behaviour.
- **`GPaste2.Add` under lock returned in 102 ms** (rc=0, history moved, no rollback after 2 s).
  Against v3.4 §1.4's **1.37 s flat** for a novel `Add` on an unlocked session, the order-of-magnitude
  gap says the locked call takes a **different path inside GPaste**, so "the history moved and did
  not roll back" does **not** establish that the Wayland selection was set. Paste-ability of an
  `Add` performed under lock is *unknown*, and §5 of this spec is written so nothing rests on it.

## 2. What the design already promises, and where it is not kept

The design doc's lifecycle section promises exactly the wanted semantics: *"Incoming clips are
accepted but not applied; only the most recent one is retained. Replaying a queue of older clips
into GPaste history once the session appears is noise, not a feature"*
(`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-07-30-clipwire-design.md:117-119`). The machinery exists and is
tested: `PHASE_PENDING`, the single latest-wins `pending_clip` slot (`agent/clipwire-agent.py:317-322,
389-397`), the drain in `clipboard_became_ready` (`:463-468`), the deferred peer-state reconcile
(`:399-406, 480-483`).

It is never reached during a lock for one reason: readiness is
`os.path.exists(wayland_socket_path())` (`:854-855`), and the socket exists throughout. A locked
session presents as `ready`, so clips are applied into a compositor that cannot complete them.

**The whole of v3.5 is: fold the lock into readiness, and repair the two places that misbehave when
a read fails while the gate is open.** No new phase, no new queue, no wire-format change, no Swift
change.

## 3. The gate

### 3.1 Readiness is socket ∧ ¬locked

`WaylandClipboard.ready()` becomes: the Wayland socket exists **and** the graphical session is not
locked. `run()`'s existing recheck loop (`:681-688`, sampled every `CLIPBOARD_RECHECK_SECONDS` = 1.0)
then maps lock → `clipboard_lost` and unlock → `clipboard_became_ready` with **no changes to the
transitions themselves**. Everything below falls out of machinery that already exists:

- Clips of **both kinds — text included** — arriving while locked land in `pending_clip`,
  latest-wins. Text is not exempted to the `Add` tier: (a) a day of immediate `Add`s is the same
  incident in a different coat — hundreds of history entries evicting the owner's own history
  toward its cap, against a session where nobody can paste; (b) exempting text would make §1's
  unknown — paste-ability under lock — load-bearing, and it is cheaper to not depend on it than to
  prove it.
- At unlock, exactly one clip — the freshest — is applied through whatever write path is current
  (the v3.4 tier once it ships; `wl-copy` until then).
- The watcher is torn down for the duration (`clipboard_lost`, `:508-512`), so the 30-second safety
  poll stops forking `wl-paste` at a lock screen. The overnight timeout bursts and the post-unlock
  30 s-cadence mutter stack-position assertions stop with it. (Correlation for the assertions, not
  proven causation — but the forks provably stop.)
- A connection made *while* locked starts in `clipboard-pending` and stays there — which also stops
  today's connect-under-lock seed read (`:458`) from eating its timeout against a hung `wl-paste`.
- `_event_source_degraded` already survives lost/ready cycles by design (`:330-333`); lock/unlock
  cycling inherits that for free.

### 3.2 The lock signal: logind `LockedHint`, event-driven, fail-open

One subscriber per agent process, holding a cached tri-state `locked ∈ {True, False, unknown}`
that `ready()` reads without blocking. `ready()` treats only `True` as locked — `unknown` is open
(fail-open), so a machine where none of this works behaves exactly as today.

It borrows the GPaste pump's **transport discipline** (`gdbus monitor`, a pump thread, pdeathsig,
`terminate()` on stop — `:1390-1426`) and must have the **opposite lifecycle**: the GPaste pump is
born in `watcher.start()` and dies in `watcher.stop()`, which under this spec means it dies at
every lock (`clipboard_lost` tears the watcher down). A lock monitor with that lifecycle is dead
by the time the unlock it exists to observe arrives, and the gate wedges closed until the next
reconnect. So: started once from `main()`'s real-clipboard branch, stopped only when the agent
process exits, and **required to survive `clipboard_lost`** — it is the unlock's only observer.
(The first draft said "like the GPaste pump it mirrors"; review caught that the analogy points
implementation at exactly the wrong lifecycle.)

The subscriber watches the **system** bus (`org.freedesktop.login1` — a different bus from every
D-Bus call the agent makes today; `loginctl` from the ssh session reaches it without extra
environment, verified today by this cycle's own probes):

1. **Resolve the graphical session**: sessions with `Class=user` and `Type` ∈ {`wayland`, `x11`}.
   Exactly one candidate → use it. Zero or more than one → `unknown`, log once, done. This machine
   has exactly one (seat0, tty2, verified today); a multi-seat future earns complexity when it
   arrives.
2. **Subscribe first, then read**: `PropertiesChanged` on that session object, *then* an initial
   `Get` of `LockedHint` — in that order, so no transition can fall between them.
3. **Re-resolve** on `SessionNew`/`SessionRemoved` (relogin changes the object path). **And
   self-heal on pump death**: if the `gdbus monitor` process exits for any reason, re-spawn it and
   re-resolve, with a bounded backoff — this is the behavioural cover for a logind or bus restart,
   whatever such a restart actually does to an existing monitor. (The first draft claimed "logind
   restart drops every subscription silently" and prescribed watching `NameOwnerChanged`; review
   flagged both halves as inferred-and-likely-wrong — `NameOwnerChanged` is emitted by the bus
   daemon, which a monitor scoped to `--dest org.freedesktop.login1` may never see. Retracted.
   What replaces it is a *requirement with a measurement*, §7.7: after
   `systemctl restart systemd-logind`, lock transitions must keep arriving.)
4. The subprocess carries the same `pdeathsig` discipline as the GPaste monitor (`:1395`) — it must
   not outlive the agent. This is the #11 leak class; one line prevents it.

**Why this is the crux and not plumbing:** the agent itself lives in an *ssh* logind session, whose
`LockedHint` is `no` forever. Subscribe to "our own session" and the gate never closes — while
fail-open makes that failure **silent**: behaviour degrades to exactly today's, no error anywhere.
The wrong-session mistake is the likeliest implementation error and is invisible in production by
construction. §7 therefore demands a regression test that pins the session *selection*, red when
the resolver picks a session whose `Class` is not `user` or whose `Type` is neither graphical kind.

**Rejected: probing writability with a throwaway `wl-copy`.** The probe is the disease — it spawns
the very focus-stealing surface this project spends two specs removing, once per probe interval,
forever.

### 3.3 Edges: debounce one way, not the other

- **Lock takes effect immediately** — the next `run()` sample flips to `clipboard-pending`.
- **Unlock opens the gate only after `LockedHint=false` has been held for one second of wall
  time** (monotonic, measured from the subscriber's transition event). Not "two consecutive
  `run()` samples", as a first draft said: `run()`'s `select` (`:667`) returns instantly while
  frames are arriving, so sample-counting collapses to milliseconds at exactly the moment it
  matters — an unlock with a clip in flight. `LockedHint` transitions arrive from a screen-shield
  animation that can bounce; the cost of the debounce is one second on a human-scale event, and
  what it buys is bounding the mid-flush re-lock race: at most one write can be in flight when a
  lock lands, so at most **one** resident `wl-copy` (image path) survives into the locked stretch —
  and today's measurement says it resolves at the next unlock on its own. Pending clips arriving
  meanwhile re-coalesce; nothing compounds. A fresh start that has never observed a lock pays no
  debounce: this is an edge rule, not a startup tax.
- The flush itself is the existing `clipboard_became_ready` body; it already no-ops on an empty
  slot.

## 4. Announcing without a readable clipboard — the tri-state, and silence under the gate

Two distinct repairs, one boundary.

### 4.1 Under a closed gate: silence, by the same precedent as before login

While locked, the agent is in `clipboard-pending` and — like every pre-login connection since the
lifecycle was designed — **announces nothing**. The Mac's announcement is stashed
(`_pending_peer_clip_state`, `:403-405`) and reconciled at unlock by the existing deferred path
(`:480-483`). Alternatives considered and rejected:

- **Announce from the store while locked** invites the Mac to reconcile against a peer that cannot
  act: a `sendMine` verdict on the PC's side needs a fresh read (`:427-430`) that hangs at a lock
  screen, failing as "clipboard changed before the send" — a reconcile that *looks* completed and
  did nothing. Deferral is honest; a stale handshake is not.
- Silence also removes the **resend amplifier** for the locked case outright: no announcement, no
  `sha256 = null`, no `sendMine`-per-reconnect loop. (`Freshness.swift:311`'s "a locked PC against
  an ordinary Mac, which happens daily" describes exactly the announce this section stops sending.)

### 4.2 Under an open gate: a failed read is not an empty clipboard

The remaining callers of a fresh read at announce time (`resolve_current_clip_state`, `:787-801`)
run with the gate open — connect on an unlocked-but-dark session, or any transient `wl-paste`
failure. Today `read()` collapses "the clipboard is empty" and "the read failed" into one `None`
(`:888-913`), and the announce path then **persists** the resulting null over the good stored
record (`announce_clip_state`, `:804-818` — `save_clip_state(*resolved)` runs unconditionally).
That clobber is what turns one unreadable moment into a resend on every subsequent reconnect: the
store can no longer rescue the next announcement either.

The boundary becomes tri-state — **content / definitely-empty / unknown** — and the announce path
treats them so:

- **content**: today's behaviour, unchanged.
- **definitely-empty** — the compositor **answered** "nothing": the listing succeeded and offered
  no usable kind, **or** it exited with wl-clipboard's no-selection signature — expected to be a
  non-zero exit with `No selection` on stderr (**expectation from wl-clipboard's behaviour, to be
  measured on this machine before the boundary ships**; the measured signature replaces this
  sentence). Today's behaviour — announce null, persist null. An empty clipboard is a fact, not a
  failure, and it MUST keep announcing null: the freshness rule's recovery case
  (`Freshness.swift:308-319`, "hands a peer back the clipboard it lost") exists precisely so an
  empty PC gets re-seeded by the Mac — a boundary that files genuine emptiness under "unknown"
  would silence that recovery for the most common benign state there is, a fresh login. (Caught in
  review; the first draft's boundary did exactly that.)
- **unknown** (spawn failure, timeout, any other failure shape): announce **from the store** if
  one exists — the true recorded age, which is the whole point of v2 freshness — and **never write
  the store**. No store either → announce null, still without persisting.

The two failure shapes measured so far do not collide: a locked session times out (rc=124 at the
3 s cap, 2026-08-17) — unknown; a genuinely empty clipboard answers fast with its own signature —
definitely-empty.

`resolve_startup_state` (`:768-775`) keeps its shape; what changes is that "unknown" never reaches
it as a fabricated `(None, now)`.

## 5. The race window, closed by reassert rather than by proof

With the gate in place, a write can still land under lock through exactly one door: the lock
arrives between `ready()`'s last sample and the write (≤ ~1 s). For `wl-copy` the measurement
already answers it: the process waits and completes at the next unlock — the resident process *is*
the retry. For the v3.4 `Add` tier the answer is unknown (§1: 102 ms, wrong path, paste-ability
unproven), so:

**On `clipboard_became_ready`, if the last completed tier write falls inside the locked interval
just ended, re-assert it: `Select(uuid)` of that history item** — sub-ms (v3.4 §1.4), uuid on argv
is not clip content (v3.4 §3.3's rule is about *bodies*), idempotent against a selection that was
in fact set. The reassert arms echo suppression the same way the write it repeats did.

**This is an explicit amendment to v3.4, named because its §3.1 interface cannot serve it:**
`write_text(body) -> bool` returns no uuid, so `Select(uuid)` has nothing to select. The tier must
record `(uuid, completion time)` of its last *confirmed* write — the uuid is already in hand at
the read-back step (v3.4 §3.4), so this is bookkeeping, not a new call. And the clocks are pinned:
the locked interval's bounds and the write-completion times both come from `time.monotonic()`
stamped at the subscriber's transition events and at write completion — never from `run()` sample
counts, which §3.3 already retired for the same reason.

Two measurements are prerequisites for implementing this section, both cheap with this cycle's
`loginctl` harness, both currently unknown:

1. **Paste-ability of an `Add` performed under lock**: add unique text under lock → unlock →
   `wl-paste -n`. If it matches, the reassert is belt-and-braces and stays anyway (the window
   exists regardless of which GPaste path ran). If it does not match, the reassert is the fix.
2. **The `Update` trace of dedup-`Add` and of `Select`**: v3.4 §3.5 measured novel writes only
   (one `Update` each, wl-copy and Add alike). Whether the 0.4 ms dedup path and a `Select` raise
   an `Update` at all decides how the reassert's echo arming is keyed — an unconsumed one-shot
   swallows the owner's next real PC-side copy as an echo, which is a lost clip, the failure class
   this project treats as worse than any blink. It sits beside v3.4's own named unknown (its §8
   last paragraph, the timeout-then-fallback `Update` trace) — not the same question, but the same
   instrument, and one measurement session should answer both.

## 6. Observability

- Lock: `session locked; holding clips (latest wins)` — once per transition.
- Unlock: `session unlocked after <duration>; N clips arrived while locked, applied the newest`
  (N counted per locked stretch, 0 included, counter reset on flush). The next incident should be
  readable from this log alone, not from tiles.
- Fail-open: one line naming what could not be resolved (`no unique graphical session: lock gate
  inactive, readiness is socket-only`), once per *transition into* fail-open — a relogin can pass
  through a two-candidate moment more than once, and the second entry must not be mute — this is
  the silent-degradation path of §3.2 made loud.
- The reassert logs the uuid it re-selected and why (`tier write completed inside a locked
  interval`).

## 7. Acceptance

Each criterion verified **by breaking it** (v3.4 §6.7 discipline — the reversion named, the test
shown red against it):

1. **Incident replay**: lock the session, send N ≥ 3 clips from the Mac (mixed text/image), unlock.
   Exactly one write occurs, it is the newest clip, **at most one** `wl-copy` remains — the flush's
   own resident selection owner, which is `wl-copy`'s documented contract (`:941-943`) and not a
   leak — against the incident's one-per-clip pile; zero "Unknown" tiles beyond that owner's;
   GPaste history grows by ≤ 1. ("Zero processes" was the first draft's criterion; it is red
   against a *correct* implementation until someone measures how long GPaste takes to re-take the
   selection after a write — nobody has.) Red when the gate is reverted to socket-only readiness.
2. **Connect under lock**: agent starts in `clipboard-pending`, announces nothing, stashes the
   Mac's announcement, reconciles once at unlock — **and the reconcile is judged by its outcome,
   not its occurrence: the Mac's fresher clip is actually pasteable on the PC after the unlock.**
   No `sha256=null` announcement is ever sent and the store is byte-identical across the locked
   window. A second run of the same criterion kills the agent mid-lock and reconnects: the newest
   clip must still arrive after unlock, through the store-and-reconcile path — pending lives in
   RAM and §9 names that. Red when 4.1 is reverted.
3. **Unreadable-but-open** (dark screen / injected read failure): the announcement carries the
   stored record with its true age; the store file is not written. Red when 4.2's unknown-branch is
   collapsed back into null. The "definitely-empty" branch keeps today's behaviour — a genuinely
   empty clipboard still announces and persists null (its own test, so the tri-state cannot quietly
   become a two-state).
4. **Wrong-session regression**: the resolver, offered this machine's real session table (four ssh
   sessions, one manager, one graphical — captured today), must pick session 2; a resolver that
   picks any `Class≠user` or non-graphical session goes red. Fail-open path: with zero and with two
   graphical candidates, readiness degrades to socket-only *and* the §6 line is logged.
5. **Bounce and race**: unlock observed for one sample then re-lock — `became_ready` must not have
   fired (debounce); a write in flight when lock lands leaves at most one resident `wl-copy`, and
   the next unlock clears it (measurement 2026-08-17 pins the clearing; the test pins the "at most
   one").
6. **Reassert**: with the tier present, a tier write timestamped inside a locked interval is
   followed at unlock by `Select` of its uuid, echo armed per §5's measurement 2; red when the
   reassert is deleted. Without the tier, no reassert path executes at all.
7. **Subscriber lifecycle**: the logind pump dies with the agent (pdeathsig — red the same way the
   GPaste pump's death is pinned) and **survives what the watcher does not**: across a
   lock/unlock/lock cycle — `clipboard_lost` tearing the watcher down twice — the same monitor
   instance keeps answering, red when it is torn down with the watcher (§3.2's lifecycle trap).
   Pump death self-heals: kill the `gdbus monitor` process, transitions resume within the backoff.
   And the logind-restart measurement: `systemctl restart systemd-logind` on the live PC, then a
   lock/unlock — the transitions must keep arriving (by surviving or by the self-heal; the
   measurement decides which mechanism carried it, and its result replaces §3.2.3's expectation).
   Re-resolves on relogin (`SessionNew` after the old session's removal).

## 8. Ships in what order, with what

Three separately landable units, one dependency:

1. **The gate** (§3) — standalone, strictly better alone: it ends the pile-up with today's
   `wl-copy` write path untouched.
2. **The tri-state announce** (§4) — standalone; it ends the store clobber and the resend loop for
   every unreadable-clipboard case, locked or dark.
3. **The reassert** (§5) — depends on the v3.4 tier existing *and* the gate existing; a no-op until
   both ship. Its two measurements happen before it, with the harness from this cycle.

v3.4 itself ships per its own §7 (write direction first); nothing here reorders it. Gate before or
after the v3.4 write tier — either order is safe; what may not happen is the reassert shipping
before its two measurements are taken.

## 9. Accepted residuals, named so they are not rediscovered as defects

1. **The Mac keeps sending while the PC is locked.** The gate is agent-side; every copy still
   crosses the wire and is dropped into a slot that holds one. Latest-wins is the design doc's own
   rule; the bandwidth is LAN bandwidth; the Mac needs no knowledge of the PC's session state and
   gains nothing from it.
2. **A clip both copied and superseded during one locked stretch never reaches the PC** — by
   design, same rule.
3. **Reads on a dark-but-unlocked session still time out** (v3.3's measurement, reproduced today).
   The gate keys on `LockedHint` because writes do; no read behaviour changes in this cycle,
   including `clipboard_became_ready`'s seed read eating its timeout when unlock happens onto a
   dark screen.
4. **Images still blink when applied on an unlocked session** (v3.4 §2.2's residual, untouched) —
   what this spec removes is their *accumulation* under lock, not their blink.
5. **`LockedHint` is trusted.** A session that wedges in a way logind does not report as locked
   (the v3.3 dark-monitor class) leaves writes on today's behaviour exactly. The gate narrows the
   failure surface; it does not claim to cover states nobody has measured a signal for.
6. **The one-second sampling cadence is inherited from `run()`**, so a copy in the first second of
   a lock can still spawn one resident `wl-copy`. Bounded by §3.3, cleared at unlock by §1's
   measurement.
7. **On a dark-but-unlocked session, §4.2's announce-from-store can produce a `sendMine` verdict
   the PC cannot execute**: the pre-send fresh read (`:427-430`) fails against the dark screen and
   the reconcile ends as "clipboard changed before the send" — completed-looking, having moved
   nothing, until the screen is lit and a later reconcile or live copy carries it. The owner will
   meet this; it is the cost of announcing true state from a machine that cannot currently read
   its own clipboard, and it is strictly better than the clobber it replaces.
8. **An agent killed mid-lock forgets the clips it was holding** — `pending_clip` and the stashed
   peer state live in RAM. Designed recovery, not loss: the next connection reconciles from the
   stores at unlock and the Mac's fresher clip is re-sent then (§7.2's second run pins exactly
   this path).

## 10. Evidence and provenance

Measurements of 2026-08-17: on the target PC over ssh, session cycled with
`loginctl lock-session 2` / `unlock-session 2` (password-free as the session owner — the harness
future cycles should reuse). Agent and Mac daemon stopped for the duration; stand restored after
(test items deleted via `GPaste2.Delete(uuid)`, daemon rebootstrapped, verified `up`).

- wl-copy pile-up, 1:1, LIFO resolution at unlock: direct observation, two spawns replicating
  `:941-965` exactly.
- `Add` under lock 102 ms / no rollback; `Select` setting the real selection (55 targets listed by
  a subsequent `wl-paste`): direct observation. Paste-ability of the locked `Add`: **not
  established** — §5 measurement 1.
- Write/read asymmetry (unlock edge vs lit screen): direct observation of the same unlock.
- Incident forensics (22 h single connection, silent log, zero leftover processes twelve minutes
  after unlock, history at its 1000 cap): Mac log `~/.local/state/clipwire/clipwire.log` and PC
  journal, 2026-08-16/17.

Inferred, and marked as such where used: that the 102 ms locked `Add` bypasses the 1.37 s
selection wait (§1 — from the timing gap alone); that the safety-poll's `wl-paste` forks cause the
30 s-cadence mutter assertions (§3.1 — correlation); the empty-clipboard `wl-paste` signature
(§4.2 — expectation until measured).

**What batya's review of this spec changed, recorded because the corrections are the useful part:**

- §4.2's "definitely-empty" boundary filed a genuinely empty clipboard — a non-zero `wl-paste`
  exit — under "unknown", which would have silenced the freshness recovery
  (`Freshness.swift:308-319`) for the most common benign state, a fresh login. Redrawn around the
  no-selection signature, with the measurement named.
- §3.2 described the monitor as "like the GPaste pump it mirrors" — a pump whose lifecycle dies in
  `watcher.stop()`, i.e. at the first lock, leaving nobody to observe the unlock. Rewritten:
  same transport, opposite lifecycle, must survive `clipboard_lost`; §7.7 pins it red.
- §7.1 demanded "zero `wl-copy` processes remain", which is red against a correct implementation:
  the flush's own write leaves one resident owner by `wl-copy`'s documented contract. Now "at most
  one, against the incident's pile".
- §3.3's debounce counted `run()` samples, which collapse to milliseconds under frame traffic —
  exactly at an unlock with a clip in flight. Now one second of wall time from the subscriber's
  clock.
- §3.2.3's logind-restart mechanism (`NameOwnerChanged` via the scoped monitor) was inferred and
  likely unimplementable as written. Replaced with pump-death self-healing plus a live measurement
  in §7.7.
- §5 relied on a uuid v3.4's `write_text(body) -> bool` never returns; the tier amendment
  (record `(uuid, completion time)` at read-back) is now explicit, clocks pinned to monotonic.
- §7.2 accepted a reconcile that happened over one that worked; now judged by the clip actually
  arriving, plus the agent-killed-mid-lock run. §9 gained residuals 7 and 8 (the dark-screen
  `sendMine` no-op, the RAM-held pending).
- §6's fail-open line was once-per-lifetime; now once per transition into fail-open.
